Fabrication of Reproducible Anisotropic Phantoms

Boston University · Team of 4 · Sep 2025 – May 2026

Optical imaging · 3D printing · SFDI

Scleroderma hardens skin through fibrosis, replacing normal basket-weave collagen with aligned collagen bundles, and we hypothesized that this makes skin optically anisotropic. Because no phantoms or test methods existed to check anisotropy, optical tools such as SFDI were hard to test and calibrate. Our team of four set out to build a phantom with skin-like optical properties and tunable anisotropy angles that SFDI could measure, judged by whether its measurements matched known dermis values, showed detectable anisotropy, and were reproducible across samples.

We reviewed the literature, designed the phantom in CAD, set infill directions in Bambu Studio, and 3D-printed about 20 PLA prototypes, testing each on an SFDI system at 851 nm and iterating. I helped coordinate fabrication timelines and test protocols and documented our methods and results. The result is an adjustable, controlled phantom that SFDI can measure at the target angles, a platform that could support future diagnosis and monitoring of skin fibrosis and improve the accuracy of optical imaging.

Optical Probe (Optode) Casing for Blood Flow Measurement

Bio Optical & Acoustic Spectroscopy Lab, Boston University · Oct 2024 – Oct 2025

SolidWorks · Optics · Fabrication

Our spectroscopy lab measures blood flow with optical fibers, and measuring through hair, such as on a person who is not bald, requires small fibers bundled together. My task was to design a casing that holds that bundle of small fibers in place.

I designed and fabricated the casing in SolidWorks, including the fiber bundle layout, a mirror slot, and a ring, and iterated on imaging results. I also analyzed large imaging datasets in MATLAB and presented design trade-offs in weekly team meetings. The casing was designed to improve measurement accuracy by about 50%. We did not take the product further because it took too much time and cost, so we stopped after weighing that trade-off.

Cleave SNP: SNP Detection and CRISPR Guide Design

EC552 Computational Synthetic Biology, Boston University · Team of 4 · Apr 2026

Python · BLAST · React · CRISPR

Most SNP tools stop at detecting a mutation, with no simple framework that carries a patient sequence from detection to genetic correction guidance. Our team of four set out to build a web platform, using sickle cell disease (the HBB gene) as the case study, that detects disease-associated SNPs and designs ranked CRISPR guide RNAs. Our specs were queries of at least 20 bp, 100% accuracy on validated test cases, and a workflow of 7 steps or fewer.

We built BLAST alignment with sequence safety checks, SNP calling matched to known disease variants, exports to TXT, JSON, CSV, and FASTA, a guide RNA step that scores PAM sites by SNP-to-cut distance, GC content, homopolymer penalty, and off-target activity, and a React web interface. When our own gRNA module could not be validated, we used Benchling for scoring and visualization. The app detected the sickle cell variant in a test HBB segment and flagged it as pathogenic, and the workflow takes 6 steps. We traced a position that was off by one to Python indexing starting at 0, and our next steps are validating the gRNA module against gold-standard tools and improving alignment filtering.

Hangle: Hangman Meets Wordle

EC327 Introduction to Software Engineering, Boston University · Team of 6 · Fall 2024

JavaScript · Git · Agile

Our software engineering course asked each team to build a web app, with a Hangman game that supports solo and two-player modes as the minimum and extra credit for added features. Our team of six set out to combine Hangman and Wordle into one polished game.

I implemented modular, object-oriented JavaScript for the game logic, animations, and user interaction, and the team used Git, agile practices, and code reviews. We delivered a working web app, presented with a live demo, with Easy, Medium, and Hard levels, a two-player mode, hints, extra lives, win and loss animations, dark mode, a challenge mode, and a sound toggle. It supports both letter guessing with a hanging man and Wordle-style word guessing.

Effects of Caffeine on Heart Rate Recovery

Systems Physiology · Summer 2024

In a physiology course, I designed and ran a human-subject experiment on how caffeine affects heart rate recovery. We followed a defined protocol, collected data with LS4 software and plethysmography, and analyzed it with a two-way correlated ANOVA and paired t-tests, then reported the statistical significance of the results.
